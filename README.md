# The Hebrew Scriptures — an English Rendering

A verse-by-verse rendering of the Hebrew Bible, kept close to the letters.

## The Hebrew comes first

This rendering exists to bring you toward the Hebrew, not to stand in
for it. The Hebrew — its consonants, its vowel points, its cantillation
— is the thing being read; the English beneath is a window, not a
replacement. Where the two seem to part, the Hebrew is right.

## The Name

Where the four-letter Name of God stands in the text, we write **YHWH**
— the consonants as they are, without substituting a title or a
vowelled form. The Name is left as the Name, in every place, so you
always know where it sits on the page.

## The aleph-tav — ⟨את⟩

You will see **⟨את⟩** kept in the English. In Hebrew, אֵת is a small
word that marks the definite direct object — it points at the thing
being acted upon and has no English equivalent, so most translations
drop it in silence. We keep it visible, in brackets, because it is part
of the sentence's structure, and we would rather show you it is there
than quietly remove it.

## Words in ⟨brackets⟩

Angle brackets ⟨like this⟩ mark words that are **not in the Hebrew** but
are needed for the English to stand — the "is," the "are," the articles
and connectives Hebrew leaves unspoken. Rather than blend them in
invisibly, we bracket them, so the Hebrew's own words are always
distinct from the ones English asks for.

## Names and titles

Where the Hebrew uses its own words for God — **Elohim**, **El**,
**Shaddai**, **Adonai** — we keep them as they stand rather than
flattening them all to "God." Each carries its own weight, and keeping
them lets you see which one the text actually used.

## Each verse in its own light

Every verse is rendered for what it says where it stands — read as the
strict monotheism of the Hebrew, one God — without importing later
doctrine or reading the end of the story back into its beginning. The
aim is to let the words mean what they mean, in place.

## The shape of the text

One file per verse: the verse itself, and a word-by-word rendering
aligned to the Hebrew, so each Hebrew word can be read against its
English.

reason for the change.
